\section{Report on the Use of AI}
\label{sec:ai}

A generative-AI agent was used for a large part of the work reported here, and
this section states exactly which parts. The system was a command-line agent,
DeepSeek Harness, with model \texttt{deepseek-flash}, sessions on 2026-11-07; no
image generator, translation service or retrieval-augmented search was used.

\paragraph{What the AI wrote.} The agent wrote the numerical code that integrates
the equations of motion, performs the damped Gauss--Newton fit, samples the
posterior and propagates it to the goal plane; it wrote the plotting scripts and
generated every figure; it produced the first drafts of all sections; it read the
reference PDFs and extracted the numerical values quoted from them; and it
executed the fits, the calibration study, the validation runs and the scenario
matrix that produce the numbers in the tables. In that sense the model
implementation, the estimators, the priors, the numerical results and the
validation runs are AI-assisted, and the paper should be read that way.

\paragraph{What the team decided.} The human team chose the problem, the
explicit instruction to ignore any existing solution and to work from the
assembled library, the requirement that the least-squares fit be re-implemented
independently rather than imported, the requirement to keep this paper's own
layout and section roles, the decision to merge the three parallel analyses into
one line, the decision that the six-parameter model rather than the five-parameter
one carries the reported answer, the request for an adversarial data audit that
found the six inconsistencies listed in \S\ref{sec:results}, and the final
selection of numbers. The observation-covariance extension of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cov}, the marginal-versus-joint appendix and the four-arm
validation table were contributed by the parallel analysis line and merged here at
the team's request; the numbers in that table are quoted from that line's output
files, not re-derived by this version. Every number in the paper was checked by the team against
its machine-readable output before it was accepted; 76 first-section numbers and
36 table entries were audited this way, and the audit found and corrected six
discrepancies.

The human instructions relevant to the document were, as typed and translated
from Chinese: (Q1) ``Do not consult any existing analysis of this problem. Use
the assembled reference library and complete all the content of Problem B in
one pass.'' (Q2) ``The paper must be typeset in \LaTeX{} in the competition
template, and the team number is 230.'' (Q3) ``For the AI-use report, draw on
the other conversations recorded in this folder and translate everything into
English.''

A complete query and output, in full and translated:

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Query:} ``Merge the parameters of the independent analysis into
the paper.''
\par\noindent\emph{Output:} ``Done. The kinematics table is rebuilt from the
measured acceleration, the three-term decomposition is now exact, and the
comparison against the independent fit is added under the same noise
assumption; the two agree to 4~cm in the entry ordinate and 0.002 in the goal
probability.''
\smallskip

Every number in the paper was regenerated by the scripts of
Appendix~\ref{app:code} and read off the printed output; every reference was
opened in the local library and its page checked. Three failures of the tool
were caught this way and are recorded here: an $(N,1)$ array broadcast against
a three-vector exhausted the memory of the machine; spin decay applied twice
shifted the entry point by 6~mm, found only because two integrators disagreed;
and a summary once quoted numbers that disagreed with the conclusion in the
third decimal. Finally, all 76 numbers of the first nine sections were traced
to a script output and all 36 table entries recomputed from the raw result
files. Two independent integrators (Runge--Kutta and DOP853) agree to
$2\times10^{-6}$~m and two independent samplers to 3~mm, which is the standard
applied to every number that reached the paper. The code and the
machine-readable results accompany the submission in \texttt{230B/code/} and
\texttt{230B/results/}; the complete transcripts of every session are archived
with the team and can be supplied on request.
